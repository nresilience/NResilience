# Stress suite: NResilience vs Polly

- Runtime: .NET 10.0.0 on macOS 26.6.1, 10 logical cores
- Matrix: full - 90 cells x 3 repetitions = 270 runs, fresh process per run, shuffled order
- Method: a 5-second steady window after a 2-second warmup, full GC between them
- Wall clock: 32.4 minutes
- Latency resolution: histogram buckets grow 1.08x, so percentiles closer than that are not separable
- Every figure is the median of the repetitions; `+-` is half the observed range as a percentage of the median

> 
> `Alloc/s` is bytes allocated per second - the quantity collection frequency actually tracks,
> and not the same ranking as B/op once the arms differ in throughput. `Pause each` is the

> allocate at the same rate and still not pay the same for it.
> 
> `Att/op` is counted only in the arms whose callback can fail, because counting it in the
> others would mean wrapping the shared gate and charging those arms for the wrapper. It reads
> `-` elsewhere; every such arm makes exactly one attempt per op, which its zero failure count
> already establishes.

## Latency: open loop at a fixed arrival rate

Ops are launched on a schedule and never wait for their predecessors, so a slower arm does not
get a lighter load. Latency is measured from each arrival's *due* time, so queueing counts. Both
arms of a pair are offered the identical absolute rate, which is what makes the comparison one
of the arms rather than of their throughputs.

> [!NOTE]
> `Lag P99` is the pacer's self-report: the 99th percentile of the gap between an arrival's due
> time and its launch. A lag comparable to the latency being measured means the row describes
> the driver rather than the arm, so it is the first column to read. Any row that missed or shed
> arrivals is listed as untrustworthy at the top of this report and is not quotable at all.
> 
> The `range` columns are the lowest and highest value the repetitions produced, and they are
> the point of this table as much as the medians are. Read them before reading any percentile:
> where a range spans orders of magnitude, the figure beside it is noise from this machine and
> not a property of the arm, and no comparison may be drawn from it.
> 
> Per-op allocation is not reported here: the open-loop driver allocates a continuation per
> arrival, so B/op in this mode includes the driver. Allocation figures come from the closed-loop
> section, where the driver allocates nothing per op.

## Retry budget under sustained failure: open loop (behavioral, not a matched A/B)

Polly has no retry budget, so this contrasts a client that caps its retries against one that
does not. Open loop, and it has to be: a refused call pauses before it reports, so under a
closed loop the budgeted arm's own pause would reduce the load it offers and every column would
measure the pause instead of the budget.

**Retries per op is the column that matters.** It is the amplification a dependency feels, and it
does not move with how fast the client happens to be running.

> rate-limit header reads, nested-retry stamping) - the B/op gap between it and `http-lib`
> prices that feature set as a package.
